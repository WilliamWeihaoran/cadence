Cadence

Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 24, 2026

Overview

Cadence is designed to keep your planning data under your control. The app stores your tasks, notes, lists, goals, habits, links, and settings locally on your device and may sync that data through your private iCloud database when iCloud sync is available.

Cadence does not sell your data and does not use your data for advertising or tracking.

Data Cadence Stores

Cadence may store the content you create in the app, including tasks, subtasks, task bundles, notes, images you paste or drop into a note, tags, documents, saved links, lists, contexts, projects, goals, habits, focus information, and calendar-link metadata.

If you choose to sign in with Apple on macOS, Cadence stores the Apple user identifier and any name or email address Apple returns to the app. Sign in with Apple is a macOS feature, so no Cadence account profile is stored on iPhone or iPad. This identity is optional and can be removed by signing out or by deleting your Cadence account and data in Settings.

Calendar Access

Cadence requests Calendar access so it can show calendar events and create, update, or delete calendar events when you ask it to — for example when you drag out a new event on the timeline or calendar board, or edit or remove an event that already exists on the calendar. These calendar events are independent of your Cadence tasks: Cadence does not attach a task to a calendar event. Calendar events are accessed through Apple's EventKit APIs. You can revoke Calendar access at any time in System Settings on macOS, or in the Settings app on iPhone and iPad.

Reminders Access

Cadence requests Reminders access so it can show your incomplete Apple Reminders in the Cadence Inbox and mark one complete when you check it off there. Reminders are accessed through Apple's EventKit APIs, on both macOS and iPhone and iPad, and appear in Settings, Reminders as well as in the Inbox.

Marking a reminder complete is the only change Cadence makes to your reminders, and only in a reminders list that allows changes. Cadence never creates a reminder, never edits a reminder's title, notes, or dates, and never deletes one. Your reminders stay in Apple's Reminders app: Cadence reads them for display and does not copy them into its own store or sync them through iCloud with your Cadence data.

Reminders access is requested separately from Calendar access, and granting one does not grant the other. It is optional, and Cadence works without it. You can revoke Reminders access at any time in System Settings on macOS, or in the Settings app on iPhone and iPad.

iCloud Sync

Cadence may use Apple's CloudKit service to sync app data through your private iCloud database. Private iCloud data is associated with your Apple account and is handled by Apple according to Apple's iCloud terms and privacy practices.

So that Cadence learns about changes made on your other devices, CloudKit sends the app silent push notifications through Apple's push service. These carry no alert, sound, or badge, and no server operated by Cadence sends them or receives them. Cadence sends no other push notifications. Reminders about your own tasks and habits are scheduled locally on your device instead, and Cadence asks for notification permission only when you turn them on in Settings, Notifications.

AI Features

Cadence includes optional AI actions that use OpenAI. These features require you to provide your own OpenAI API key, which Cadence stores in Keychain. Cadence sends selected note content to OpenAI only when you run an AI action, such as summarizing a note or extracting task drafts.

OpenAI processes those requests according to OpenAI's policies and the account associated with your API key. You can remove the saved API key from Cadence Settings at any time.

Diagnostics and Backups

Cadence can create local backups of its store before migration or restore work. These backups remain on your device. Cadence may show local error messages to help you understand backup, restore, Calendar, AI, or sync problems.

Account and Data Deletion

On macOS you can delete your Cadence account and data in the app from Settings, Account or Settings, Data Safety. On iPhone and iPad the same deletion is in Settings, Data Safety.

Either route removes Cadence-created content from the current store, removes local Cadence backups and pending restores, and removes the saved OpenAI API key. On macOS it also clears the local Apple account profile; on iPhone and iPad there is no Cadence account profile to clear. Because Cadence syncs through your private iCloud database, these deletions reach your other devices.

Deletion always asks you to confirm first, and it cannot be undone.

Apple Calendar events that already exist in Calendar are managed by Calendar. You can delete those events directly in Calendar or through Cadence calendar features where available.

Exporting Your Data

Settings, Data Safety on macOS, iPhone and iPad offers Export an Archive. It writes a single JSON file containing everything Cadence stores — tasks, lists, notes, goals, habits, tags, saved links and embedded images — which you can open in any text editor and keep wherever you choose. The file leaves Cadence entirely: nothing is uploaded, and Cadence keeps no copy of where you put it.

Cadence also keeps automatic copies of its local store, but those live inside the app and are removed when you delete Cadence's data. An exported archive is the copy that outlives the app. Cadence cannot read an archive back in yet.
